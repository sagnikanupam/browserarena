The Great Wall's history spans from the 7th century BC to the present. Initially, separate states built fortifications during the Spring and Autumn (771–476 BC) and Warring States periods (475–221 BC). Qin Shi Huang, the first emperor, connected many of these walls in the 3rd century BC using rammed earth and forced labor to defend against northern nomads. 

Key dynasties contributed:
*   **Qin Dynasty (221–206 BC):** Connected existing walls.
*   **Han Dynasty (206 BC–220 AD):** Extended the wall westward to protect the Silk Road.
*   **Northern Qi (550–574) & Sui Dynasty (581–618):** Undertook massive construction efforts.
*   **Tang (618–907), Song (960–1279), Yuan (1271–1368), and Qing (1644–1912):** Largely refrained from building, favoring other strategies. The Qing ruled territories on both sides, diminishing the wall's military role.
*   **Ming Dynasty (1368–1644):** Built the most substantial and recognizable sections, often using stone and brick, in response to Mongol threats. Despite this, the Wall was breached in 1644, allowing the Manchus (Qing) to take power.

In modern times, after periods of neglect and destruction (notably during the Cultural Revolution), the Great Wall became a national symbol and a UNESCO World Heritage site in 1987. It is now a major tourist destination with ongoing preservation efforts.
