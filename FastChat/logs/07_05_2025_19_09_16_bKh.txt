1. **USCourts.gov - Pretrial Risk Assessment (PTRA)**: The federal Pretrial Risk Assessment (PTRA) is a scientifically based instrument developed by the Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts (AO). It is used by probation and pretrial services officers to assess a defendant’s risk of failure to appear, new criminal arrests, or technical violations during pretrial. The PTRA helps officers make informed release or detention recommendations to ensure court appearances and community safety. It is integrated with pretrial investigations and evidence-based practices to reduce pretrial failure rates. Key aspects include assessing risks of non-appearance and revocation, and it is used alongside professional judgment for release suitability decisions. Numerous related articles and research from the Federal Probation Journal provide further context on its development and validation.

2. **NACDL.org - Making Sense of Pretrial Risk Assessments (June 2018, The Champion)**: This article by Brandon Buskey and Andrea Woods discusses the growing use of pretrial risk assessment tools in over 60 U.S. jurisdictions (covering 25% of the population) as a reform to reduce pretrial detention and address money bail issues. Tools like the Public Safety Assessment (PSA), PTRA, and COMPAS categorize individuals into risk groups based on historical data predicting failure to appear or re-arrest. They influence judicial decisions on release or detention but do not provide individualized predictions. Criticisms include potential racial bias (e.g., COMPAS flagged Black defendants as high risk disproportionately), accuracy limitations (30-40% error rates), lack of transparency in tool design, over-prediction of risk, and constitutional concerns regarding individualized bail determinations and the presumption of innocence.
